# Research brief: preserving the information foundation

Research cutoff: **2026-10-07**. This brief separates source findings, bounded analytical inferences, and recommendations. Source IDs link to the retained version or explicitly labeled excerpt; public URLs, publication/version dates, rights, and access gaps are in [source-catalog.jsonl](source-catalog.jsonl). Different versions and supplements are not independent replications.

## What is changing online

**The defensible finding is changing content provenance, not a measured collapse of the internet.** Pew Research Center reports that **10% of its random sample of 10,000 English-language pages from the July 2026 Common Crawl snapshot** showed significant detector-based signs of AI authorship or substantial editing. This is neither a census of internet content nor a count of inaccurate material. Its methodology sampled 49 crawls and 490,000 pages across the complete time series; those are not the July denominator. The publication-dated post-ChatGPT subset is nonrandom, so its separate 35% estimate cannot replace the July snapshot estimate. Detection is probabilistic, including possible false positives. [R011](sources/research/R011.md), opening visualization; [R012](sources/research/R012.md), “Data collection” and “Detecting AI authorship.”

The following evidence is not interchangeable:

| Evidence | What was measured | Interpretation boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Pew, August 2026 | Detector classifications of sampled publicly accessible English-language Common Crawl pages. | Authorship/editing signals do not establish factual quality; inaccessible and non-English material are not represented equivalently. R011–R012. |
| Dolezal et al., arXiv v1, April 2026 | Archived English-language webpages, detector scores, extracted claims and factual-accuracy assessment. | The study found no statistically significant macro-level association between AI prevalence and factual-accuracy decline under its measures. This observational null result is not proof of no harm or a causal experiment. [R013](sources/research/R013.md), Results/Hypothesis 2, §§3.1–3.4, Appendices A/D. |
| Social-media and Wikipedia studies | Platform-specific samples and detector-based estimates. | Different platforms, languages, dates, baselines and detection assumptions limit comparison with a whole-web estimate. [R037](sources/research/R037.md), §§3–5/Limitations; [R038](sources/research/R038.md), §§3–5/Limitations. |
| Graphite's corporate studies | Selected English articles/listicles with article-schema markup, rather than all webpages. | The October 2025 page is explicitly superseded by the May 2026 study. Their article-selection and binary-detector rules differ from Pew's. No Graphite percentage is adopted as a whole-internet measurement. [R044](sources/research/R044.md), Update/Methods; [R045](sources/research/R045.md), Selection/AI Detection/Limitations. |
| Spennemann's preprint | Search-index trends in words associated with generated text. | Keyword frequency is an indirect proxy affected by human usage and search-index behavior. Its extrapolation is not adopted as measured whole-web prevalence. [R039](sources/research/R039.md), Methodology/Results. |

In this package, **“AI slop” is only an informal label for low-quality or unverified generated content**, not a category encompassing all synthetic training data. Preserve independently originated, documented records and observations, including instrument-produced records. Do not make human authorship, a pre-2022 date, or a detector verdict a truth certificate. These are proposed collection rules, not a claim that there was an infallible pre-AI internet.

## How recursive training can fail

**Model collapse** here means degradation across model-training generations when generated outputs feed later training. The literature uses different outcomes: increasing test error, loss of distributional tails, declining diversity, and impaired scaling with more training data. Schaeffer et al.'s position paper distinguishes these definitions; its assessment of their real-world importance is an argument, not a separate experiment. [R033](sources/research/R033.md), §§1–3.

The basic feedback mechanism is conditional. An initial model approximates a source distribution. Finite sampling, imperfect fitting or generation choices can omit uncommon patterns or introduce distortions. Training a later model on those outputs can reproduce and compound the distortion. Whether this happens depends on what data are retained or added, which outputs are selected, the learning procedure, model capacity, modality and evaluation target. [R001](sources/research/R001.md), “What is model collapse?” and “Model collapse in language models”; [R076](sources/research/R076.md), §§2.1–3 and 6.

Shumailov et al.'s *Nature* study includes OPT-125m fine-tuning on WikiText-2, alongside mathematical and image-model analyses. Its language experiments compare generated-data retraining with a condition retaining a sampled portion of original data; the latter reduces degradation in that setup. The experiments do not sample deployed frontier models as a population. The authors also distinguish finite experimental runs from asymptotic theoretical results. [R001](sources/research/R001.md), journal pp. 757–758; supplement [R070](sources/research/R070.md).

The March 2025 author correction changes the stated theoretical condition from `αᵢ = γᵢ = 0` to **`βᵢ = γᵢ = 0`**. It is a correction, not a retraction; the notice does not replace the empirical results. [R002](sources/research/R002.md), p. E6.

Three mechanisms must remain separate:

- **Recursive training:** later fitting changes learned parameters using earlier models' outputs. R001's experiments address this mechanism.
- **Runtime retrieval:** a deployed system can receive poor external context without retraining its underlying model. NIST distinguishes training from deployment/inference, including retrieval-augmented generation. A fixed model does not spontaneously change its weights because the web changes; this is an inference from that distinction, not a result of the collapse experiments. [R015](sources/research/R015.md), §3.1.1, printed pp. 36–39.
- **Adversarial poisoning:** an attacker deliberately manipulates training information or another part of the learning pipeline. Accidental recursive degradation does not require such an attacker, and poisoning need not involve recursive generations. [R015](sources/research/R015.md), §2.3 and §3.2.1; R001, Introduction; R070, §1.2.

The separate June 3, 2025 NIST potential-updates notice lists proposed taxonomy-index corrections and explicitly distinguishes them from official changes. It does not withdraw the report or supply a revised edition. [R020](sources/research/R020.md), correction notice, p. 1.

Use “echo chamber” only as an analogy for feedback, not a substitute for specifying the data-generation, retention and training process. Evaluate factual accuracy and rare-case coverage separately from fluency.

## Counterevidence and conditions

### Original-data retention is useful, but is not a universal cure

Gerstgrasser et al. compare replacement with accumulation across language models, molecular diffusion and image variational autoencoders. Accumulation avoids unbounded error growth in their analyzed settings, but their CelebA experiment still loses some minor visual variation. Bounded error is therefore not the same claim as unchanged diversity. [R003](sources/research/R003.md), §§2.1–2.3 and 3, especially §2.3/Fig. 6.

Kazdan et al. distinguish **replacement**, **training on an expanding accumulated pool**, and **accumulating a pool but training on fixed-size subsamples**. The last incurs more loss than full accumulation in the reported comparisons, although loss commonly plateaus rather than diverging. Retaining bytes and allocating enough training resources to use them are different interventions. [R040](sources/research/R040.md), §§2–4/Fig. 4; [R004](sources/research/R004.md) is the related preprint, not an independent study.

Barzilai and Shamir prove consistency for accumulated-data iterative maximum-likelihood estimation under structural, regularity and sample-size conditions, even as the real-data fraction approaches zero. They also construct collapse counterexamples when required assumptions fail. This is conditional statistical theory, not a guarantee for every neural training pipeline. [R050](sources/research/R050.md), Theorem 4.1 and Theorems 5.1–5.2; related author version [R041](sources/research/R041.md).

Bertrand et al. retain the same original real set at every round while regenerating synthetic augmentation. Their local stability results require a sufficiently well-fit starting model, regularity assumptions and a sufficiently limited synthetic contribution; the finite-sample result needs an additional update-error assumption. Their experiments concern normalizing-flow and diffusion models, not autoregressive LLM pretraining. They do not establish that fresh observations are always required, or that a particular mixture works universally. [R074](sources/research/R074.md), §§2–3, 5–6.

### Verification must supply relevant information, not merely confidence

Feng et al. show that selecting generated examples using verifiers can improve results in studied mathematical and summarization tasks. A stronger general-purpose model is not automatically the better selector in their summarization experiment. [R007](sources/research/R007.md), retained arXiv v2, §§3–7.

Yi et al. analyze a verifier-induced bias–variance tradeoff: filtering can reduce variance while introducing bias, and the specified long-run regression process moves toward the verifier's knowledge center. Their experiments include MNIST conditional variational autoencoders and SmolLM2-135M summarization. The summarization verifier scores against ground-truth reference summaries; that reference access is a material condition. These are not demonstrations that arbitrary self-checking certifies open-domain truth. [R009](sources/research/R009.md), ICLR 2026 main-conference paper, §§2–6, particularly §5.3.

Gillman et al. study self-correction using target-aware mathematical correction, class-conditioned image prototypes and physics-based correction of generated human motion. Their motion-training loop retains the original real dataset. Its reported “100%” synthetic augmentation is a synthetic-to-real ratio of one-to-one, not an entirely synthetic dataset. The paper's language-model extension is future work, not an experiment. [R075](sources/research/R075.md), §§3–8, especially Definition 4.1 and §§7–8.

**Synthesis:** the evidence supports a conditional reliability concern and the value of preserving options for checking and retraining. It does not establish inevitable internet-wide collapse, a deadline for failure, or a measured effect on hypothetical superintelligence. Nor does it establish a universally safe synthetic-data fraction. Preserve access to originals, continue acquiring relevant fresh observations, and evaluate the actual training and deployment pipeline; these are complementary recommendations, not a theorem that an archive alone prevents degradation.

## What preservation and provenance can and cannot do

**Provenance** documents origin and subsequent history. NIST treats provenance, watermarking and detection as complementary approaches with important limitations. Authentic content can mislead, and a valid signature can support a statement about signer or integrity without establishing that the depicted event or asserted fact is true. [R014](sources/research/R014.md), Executive Summary and §3.1, especially §3.1.2.

The C2PA explainer likewise separates provenance validation from judgments about truth. Missing credentials need not mean that content is false, and credentials can be incomplete or removed. The retained explainer identifies itself as version 2.2, released December 11, 2025, despite the version 2.3 download path. [R035](sources/research/R035.md), cover and §§2, 7.1.2, 7.2.

Detection is neither uniformly reliable nor uniformly useless. NIST's controlled text-to-text pilot finds performance differences across tested generators and discriminators; its summarization task and binary-authorship evaluation do not establish detector reliability on every language, editing process or web corpus. [R036](sources/research/R036.md), §§7.1, 8.2–8.4 and 9.2.

Nemecek et al.'s workshop-associated study examines disagreement between signed provenance and watermark signals under particular image/credential conditions. Its self-signed certificate produced an unrecognized-issuer warning; this is not evidence that every fully trusted deployment was defeated. The joint NSA and partner-agency guidance already warns that provenance metadata alone cannot establish truth. [R047](sources/research/R047.md), §§4–6.2; [R049](sources/research/R049.md), PDF pp. 4–6.

NARA's preservation strategy provides an existing operational model: retain original objects, record transformations and preservation metadata, replicate holdings in separate geographic environments, check fixity and control access. Its program description also identifies regular backup-restoration tests. A checksum demonstrates whether bytes match a reference state; it does not prove the source's factual assertions. [R016](sources/research/R016.md), Strategies 4–6 and Activities 1–4; [R017](sources/research/R017.md), program overview.

The proposed command should preserve originals separately from cleaned, annotated and generated derivatives; maintain custody, rights and transformation records; and test restoration. Unknown origin must remain unknown. Preservation copies should complement lawful records schedules, not create an unlimited retention exception. Quality, representativeness, mission suitability and permission for a proposed use should be evaluated separately. The NDSA's 2026 level-update announcement cautions that its highest preservation level is not the goal in every area for every organization. [R034](sources/research/R034.md), version 2.1 announcement. As a policy choice, apply preservation controls proportionate to mission risk; limit collection scope separately through mission priorities and lawful authority.

## Defense relevance and existing responsibilities

Stanton's May 2025 statement places data delivery within the warfighter's mission and distinguishes DISA's infrastructure role from JFHQ-DoDIN's defensive operations. His March 2026 testimony emphasizes mission-dependent prioritization, clear responsibility and measurable readiness. Those passages establish a relevant operational frame; they do not establish his endorsement of this proposal or a position on recursive model collapse. [S001](sources/stanton/S001.md), PDF pp. 2–5; [S003](sources/stanton/S003.md), Stanton oral remarks, PDF pp. 12–14, and prepared statement, PDF pp. 16–17.

The institutional starting point is not a vacuum:

- **Existing defense information organizations:** S001 and S003 describe DISA and the operational cyber-defense command's distinct, mutually supporting responsibilities. The proposed preservation-assurance mission should complement those roles, not silently absorb them.
- **Existing records responsibilities:** the 2023 Department of Defense Records Strategy addresses curation, automation and governance of records across their lifecycle, including records schedules and transfers to NARA. Chief Information Officer and component records personnel must therefore be partners in any new design. [R046](sources/research/R046.md), §§3.1 and 4.2.
- **Existing AI/data responsibilities:** the Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Office's public mission covers data, analytics and AI adoption. The January 9, 2026 AI strategy directs component-level federated data catalogs and CDAO coordination. This is a policy instruction, not evidence that every catalog is complete or effective. [R025](sources/research/R025.md), Mission/Our Priorities; [R022](sources/research/R022.md), “Data Access,” PDF pp. 3–4.
- **Prior governance foundations:** the November 2023 adoption strategy describes distributed data-product ownership and quality/access responsibilities; the 2023 metadata guidance supplies a common baseline rather than an all-encompassing solution. The earlier strategy is historical context, not an assertion that all provisions remain unchanged after 2026 direction. [R023](sources/research/R023.md), printed pp. 8–10/Appendix A; [R024](sources/research/R024.md), memorandum and §2.
- **Civilian preservation and standards:** NARA retains its custodial mission, while NIST and the joint provenance guidance provide technical practices. R014, R016–R017, R049. Do not treat those practices and missions as a comparative evaluation of this command proposal.

GAO's live GAO-22-105834 page marks recommendations 1–6 **Closed–Implemented** and recommendation 7 **Open**. Its August 2026 update for recommendation 7 reports expected completion in April 2027. This is not proof of future completion, and the original 2022 findings must not all be presented as still unresolved. [R018](sources/research/R018.md), “Recommendations for Executive Action,” recommendations 1–7.

For the legislative proposal, use **Department of Defense**. Executive Order 14347 permits secondary “Department of War” titles while retaining controlling statutory references until law changes; the official 2024 edition of 10 U.S.C. §111(a) names the Department of Defense. [R019](sources/research/R019.md), §2; [R021](sources/research/R021.md), §111(a). Use the 2024 edition as explicitly dated context, not as verified current consolidated text. Do not infer from these general texts either that the proposed command is already authorized or that no establishment authority exists; request explicit congressional establishment and legal review of the implementing authorities.

**Defense relevance is an inference, not a documented incident:** when a mission depends on checking a rare technical condition or reconstructing why a decision was made, recoverable source records provide an option that a generated summary alone cannot guarantee. This inference connects the source-coverage limitations in R001/R003/R009 with the preservation controls in R016. As a drafting safeguard, do not turn that inference into an asserted classified incident, measured defense-model failure rate, or quantified operational saving.

## Implications for the proposed command

**Recommendation:** establish a focused military Strategic Information Command within the Department of Defense. Its proposed added value is accountable execution of a defense preservation-and-assurance mission, not ownership of all information or proof that a new organization is scientifically necessary. Do not turn the recommendation into only a study of whether to establish it; require the Secretary's implementation plan to settle internal reporting relationships, capabilities, costs and deconfliction. Do not create a new global unified combatant command.

Give it four duties:

1. **Identify and prioritize collections and custodians.** Rank existing defense-critical collections by mission dependency, uniqueness, provenance quality and risk of loss. Include original government/scientific observations, authoritative technical and historical records, and lawful defense mission datasets. Do not authorize indiscriminate collection of the civilian internet.
2. **Preserve recoverable originals.** Use versioned, access-controlled and geographically separated preservation copies, fixity records, custody metadata and restoration exercises. Separate originals from cleaned, annotated and synthetic derivatives. Do not retroactively certify unknown material as untainted.
3. **Enable authorized, traceable use.** Record origin, transformations, rights and access restrictions; distinguish observed from generated material. Use DISA infrastructure expertise and CDAO evaluation/data expertise while preserving NARA and other custodians' responsibilities. Nongovernmental participation must rest on lawful agreements, not compelled collection or general content-policing power.
4. **Demonstrate readiness.** Require independent restoration exercises and task-relevant model/data evaluations, including uncommon but consequential cases. Keep held-out evaluation material out of training. Report provenance coverage, restoration results, task-specific error/diversity changes, unresolved custody/access issues and lifecycle costs—not bytes alone.

Request three congressional actions:

1. **Authorize establishment and the narrow mission**, with named accountability, bounded collection/use authority and explicit civilian-control safeguards.
2. **Require a costed, phased implementation plan** that uses existing capabilities, identifies duplication before expansion, and includes retention, security, restoration and evaluation costs. Do not invent a funding figure, headcount or savings estimate.
3. **Require annual reporting and independent GAO/Inspector General oversight**, with civil-liberties, privacy, copyright and classification safeguards. Reports should distinguish verified readiness results from unresolved deficiencies and planned work.

These measures are proposed requirements, not current performance claims. The case for a command remains a policy judgment about operational accountability and coordination. The evidence also supports taking seriously the alternative of strengthening existing offices; it does not prove a new headquarters is always superior. The requested command model should therefore be judged against explicit mission outcomes and duplication controls, not the act of establishing an organization.
