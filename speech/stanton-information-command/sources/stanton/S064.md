## Factual Summary
ExecutiveGov covers Lt. Gen. Paul Stanton's March 24, 2026 testimony before the Senate Armed Services Subcommittee on Cybersecurity. The report summarizes Stanton's statements that military networks must be operated as critical weapons systems and that battlefield advantage depends on delivering the right data to the right place at the right time. The article highlights the role of zero-trust architecture in enabling agile, data-centric coalition environments, DCDC's elevation to a sub-unified command to execute proactive cyber campaigns against systemic vulnerabilities, and Stanton's emphasis on personnel readiness as a non-negotiable priority.

## Short Attributed Style-Anchor Excerpts

### Core Mission Task
> “In the face of determined and capable adversaries across the globe, the joint force’s success on the battlefield comes down to one core task: getting the right data to the right place at the right time to make a better and faster decision than the enemy.”

### Zero Trust as Foundation for Agile Coalitions
> “This is the essential model that enables the agile, data-centric Mission Partner Environment, allowing us to move beyond the legacy approach of building isolated hardware-defined networks for each coalition.”

### Campaigning Against Coordinated Actors
> “We are moving beyond chasing individual alerts to campaigning against systemic vulnerabilities and coordinated threat actors.”

### Readiness as Non-Negotiable Priority
> “Technology provides the tools, but it is trained and accountable people who win battles, which is why readiness is a non-negotiable, number one priority of both the command and the agency.”
