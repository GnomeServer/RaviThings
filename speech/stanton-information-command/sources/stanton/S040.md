## Factual Summary
Breaking Defense reports on remarks by Maj. Gen. Paul Stanton and Cyber School Deputy Commandant Todd Boudreau at TechNet Augusta 2023. Stanton discussed the Army's approach to adopting artificial intelligence while avoiding uncritical hype. He stated that the Army's cyber workforce development is not intended to turn soldiers into programmers who write raw code, but rather into professionals who understand that AI systems are entirely predicated on large aggregated datasets. Stanton stressed the crucial question of data provenance and dataset confidence: how can a commander or operator trust an algorithm when operating on external data that they did not generate? He announced a pilot program with Army Futures Command evaluating large language models to assist soldiers with doctrine-based operational research (e.g., executing doctrinally correct wet-gap crossings), and noted a Combined Arms Center initiative requiring representatives from every center of excellence to undergo foundational data literacy training.

### AI Predicated on Data Aggregation
> “we’re not developing someone that understands how to write code — what we are developing is someone that understands that artificial intelligence is predicated on the aggregation of a lot of data.”

### Data Provenance and Dataset Confidence
> “And so, separately, within our cyber workforce and specifically coordination with Army Cyber Command, we’re thinking a lot about what does it mean to develop confidence and an externally derived dataset upon which an AI algorithm is run. So if I’m pulling in data from lots of different places, what degree of confidence do I have in individual data set that I didn’t generate?”

### Doctrine-Support LLM Pilot
> “So if a soldier wants to ask a ChatGPT-like question that says, you know, ‘How do I execute a doctrinally correct wet gap crossing?,’ then you would get back an answer, because the large language model had surveyed all of our doctrine in order to support an informed response. We’re in the early stages of it. But my point is we’re not sitting on the sidelines and watching, we’re diving in.”
