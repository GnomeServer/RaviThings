## Factual Summary
In this published Q&A, Lt. Gen. Stanton discusses the execution of the DISA Next strategy established by his predecessor, Lt. Gen. Robert Skinner. Stanton emphasizes that his leadership focus is on measurable, tangible near-term outcomes rather than modifying overarching strategic documents. He highlights the Multi-Partner Environment (MPE) effort as an operational priority, targeting standardized hybrid cloud architectures across all 11 combatant commands so data can be aggregated and analyzed flexibly without bespoke, one-off engineering. Addressing technical debt, Stanton prioritizes DoDNet across defense agencies and field activities (DAFAs) to establish a defensible zero-trust architecture. Furthermore, he underscores that expeditionary warfighting requirements must drive capability development, balancing cost, schedule, and performance against emergent operational needs.

## Short Attributed Style-Anchor Excerpts

### Near-Term Measurable Outcomes (Skinner Strategy)
**Question:** Where is DISA in implementing the DISA Next strategy?  
> "My focus has been on the tangible and measurable outcomes on a near-term horizon that move us towards the strategic goals."

### Standardized Architecture Across Combatant Commands
**Question:** What, specifically, do you expect to measure?  
> "We need to provide them with the underlying architecture that is normalized and standardized across all 11 combat commands so that we don’t do one-offs at each one but rather provide them the environment to run the tool that they need and then give them the agility to adjust that tool."

### Technical Debt in Defense Agencies
**Question:** Where is DISA in divesting technical debt?  
> "Until we are successful, we’re going to drag that technical debt with us. We can’t afford to do that, so that’s our initial focus."

### Expeditionary Warfighting Constraints
**Question:** Will DISA 2030 capabilities change the way warfighters operate?  
> "Much of our war fight is conducted in an expeditionary sense. That makes things hard if you have to move your capability forward over great distance while the enemy is attempting to kill you. That’s a fundamentally different problem than industry has to face routinely. That’s our problem."
