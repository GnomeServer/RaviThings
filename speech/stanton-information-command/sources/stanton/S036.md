## Factual Summary
C4ISRNet reports on remarks by Brig. Gen. Paul Stanton during an August 19, 2021 media roundtable at TechNet Augusta in Augusta, Georgia. Stanton, who had recently assumed leadership of the Cyber Center of Excellence following operational tours at U.S. Cyber Command and Army Cyber Command, identified a major readiness gap: soldiers graduating from the Army Cyber School were not ready on "day one" to conduct operational missions. Instead, graduates routinely required follow-on courses and certifications before they could execute offensive or defensive cyber tasks. Stanton contrasted this with combat arms branches like infantry, where soldiers arriving at their units are immediately prepared to participate in unit training and missions. He argued that the schoolhouse must adjust its instructional models so that all necessary certification and qualification training occurs prior to graduation, and noted that readiness reporting was failing to accurately communicate this deficiency to broader Army leadership.

## Short Attributed Style-Anchor Excerpts

### Operational Readiness on Day One
> “Looking at readiness and our soldiers and our formations, our units: Are they ready to execute the missions — whether they be offense or defense — that we’re asking of them the day that they arrive to the unit? When you graduate from the schoolhouse, in my personal opinion, you should be ready to execute your job.”

### Communicating Cyber Readiness
> “When we look at our readiness numbers, we’re not effectively communicating that to the rest of the Army.”

### Reforming Schoolhouse Training Models
> “We in the schoolhouse need to figure out: How do we ensure that we adjust our models such that all of the training and education necessary to be certified to do your job is conducted in the schoolhouse? There’s a lot to that, but that’s a huge operational lesson learned that I’m bringing with me in the approach to the Cyber Center of Excellence.”
