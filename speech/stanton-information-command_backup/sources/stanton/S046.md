## Factual Summary
FedScoop reports on an initiative outlined by Maj. Gen. Paul Stanton at TechNet Augusta on August 16, 2022. Serving as executive agent for advanced cyber work-role curriculum development, the Army piloted a distributed, multi-service training curriculum for offensive cyber operations on behalf of U.S. Cyber Command. Stanton identified a readiness shortfall: service members arriving from initial training were not operational on day one. Historically, offensive training relied on legacy National Security Agency intelligence standards, which required lengthy, highly restrictive certification pipelines designed to avoid detection during espionage. Stanton argued that conventional military warfighters operate under different risk profiles and rules of engagement than intelligence agencies; military cyber forces do not all require high-end NSA foreign intelligence certifications to execute battlefield missions. The pilot established appropriate operational breakpoints to deploy qualified operators sooner, followed by modular advanced education.

## Short Attributed Style-Anchor Excerpts

### Distinguishing Military Cyber From Intelligence Espionage
> “Not every offensively oriented soldier needs to be trained to a comparable standard to execute a foreign intelligence mission on behalf of the National Security Agency.”

### Operational Breakpoints and Risk Profiles
> “We want to be able to train service members to be able to execute missions that fall into that different risk profile and be able to execute those missions earlier in their training and development than matriculating all the way through a very long, lengthy and quite difficult training model to become foreign intelligence certified on behalf of the NSA. Working in coordination with the NSA, just looking at where’s the right breakpoint, if you will, that allows a service member to then execute operations that fall under a different risk model and then subsequently continue their education to become certified at the NSA standard.”

### Joint Service Training Pipeline
> “This is a joint endeavor, in coordination with U.S. Cyber Command with the other services to produce service members that are ready to be part of their cyber mission force teams.”
