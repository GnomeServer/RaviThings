## Factual Summary
C4ISRNet examines the U.S. Army's conceptual transition from "information warfare" to the broader framework of "information advantage," aimed at enabling commanders to make better-informed, faster decisions across all domains and phases of conflict. The article features insights from Brig. Gen. Paul Stanton and Col. Michael Hammerstrom from the Cyber Center of Excellence at TechNet Augusta. Stanton explained that information advantage must extend beyond the tactical edge into service-wide doctrine, noting the development of updated cyber and electromagnetic activities doctrine intended for all soldiers, not just technical specialists. He emphasized that operationalizing information advantage requires integrating public affairs, cyber, and military information support operations (MISO) into tangible steps that nest directly within a tactical or operational commander's scheme of maneuver. Stanton also described ongoing experimental models evaluating whether information advantage responsibilities should reside within staff sections or dedicated command formations.

## Short Attributed Style-Anchor Excerpts

### Extending Doctrine to the Whole Army
> “We’re looking at what does it mean to create an ADP [Army doctrine publication] instead of an FM [field manual] that allows us to publish the relevant information sooner so that we can effectively communicate to the whole Army.”

### Integrating Multi-Disciplinary Information Capabilities
> “What does it mean to combine public affairs with cyber with MISO [military information support operations] with all of the different elements necessary to achieve conceptually what we think are objectives associated with influencing and supporting commander decision-making? What are the tangible steps that we can actually do that are then informing an operational commander?”

### Nesting in the Scheme of Maneuver
> “When we say ‘operationalizing,’ we’re really talking about achieving objectives that nest within a commander’s scheme of maneuver.”
