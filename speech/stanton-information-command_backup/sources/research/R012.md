## Data collection — factual summary (editorial)

Pew randomly sampled 10,000 English-language pages from each of 49 Common Crawl crawls between January 2021 and July 2026, totaling 490,000 pages. Publicly inaccessible, login-gated and paywalled material is underrepresented. About 10%–15% of sampled pages had a detectable publication date. The dated post-ChatGPT subset is explicitly nonrandom; its 35% figure must not replace the 10% July snapshot estimate.

## Detecting AI authorship — factual summary (editorial)

Pew applied Pangram's editlens_Llama-3.2-3B detector to extracted page body text, using a score threshold of 0.2. The threshold was calibrated to commercial Pangram 3.3 categories. A comparison on 62,370 pages from seven crawls reported 96% agreement and Cohen's kappa 0.61. This is agreement between detectors, not independently measured truth. Pew warns of probable false positives in early pre-ChatGPT crawls and of uncertain individual-page classifications.

## Source notes

Read the complete methods and footnote 1. The July 2026 denominator is 10,000, not 490,000. The latter is the full time-series sample. A page flagged by this method is neither proved machine-authored nor proved inaccurate. Preserve this methodology with R011.
