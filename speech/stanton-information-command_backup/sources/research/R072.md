**What the paper reports:** Main-text §§2–3 describe recursive fine-tuning of Gemma 3 1B IT using 8,000 64-token WikiText-2 prompts, synthetic proportions of 25%, 50%, and 100%, and 0.5 training epochs per iteration; it evaluates five MMLU subject subsets of 100 questions each. The authors report factual-accuracy decline before surface-format failure and claim a domain-aligned World Religions condition reduces decline by roughly 15× (main text §§2–3; Appendix A.2–A.7, B.1–B.3). The authors state that the study is limited to one model architecture and five subjects and call for testing at broader scales/domains (Limitations and Future Work).

**Reliability cautions:** Numerical claims are not used in the core synthesis. Section 2 and Appendix A.3 define the mixture parameter as the synthetic fraction, while Appendix A.2 describes it as the real-data fraction. Appendix A.7 describes selecting 100 short segments and packing them into 8,000 training blocks without adequately explaining that expansion. These protocol ambiguities need resolution before relying on the reported effect sizes. The study is limited to one model and five subjects. The abstract's proposed applicability to different models is not itself evidence that multiple models were tested, and is not treated here as an additional contradiction.

**Disposition:** Directly relevant as a lead about possible separation of factual accuracy and fluency, but not relied on for quantitative or causal claims in the core synthesis because the described experimental protocol and reproducibility are internally unresolved. Do not infer a general LLM, deployed-frontier, or internet-wide effect.
