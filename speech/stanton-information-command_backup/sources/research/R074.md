## Study design and method

The paper models iterative fine-tuning as repeated likelihood-based updates on a fixed empirical real-data set plus generated examples drawn from the immediately previous model. Its synthetic-to-real sample ratio is denoted λ. The synthetic set is regenerated each round; the original real set is retained in every round. The authors distinguish this fixed-data augmentation design from designs that accumulate generated samples or continually acquire newly observed real examples (Sections 2.1–2.2, 3.1, and 4).

## Findings

- **Purely generated Gaussian samples:** Proposition 1 (Section 2.2) proves contraction of expected covariance scale for a single repeatedly re-estimated multivariate Gaussian that is trained only on its generated observations. Its contraction factor approaches one as sample size increases, so larger samples slow rather than erase the collapse in that model.
- **Local stability with retained real data:** Theorem 1 (Section 3.1) gives a sufficient local-contraction condition around a well-fit maximum-likelihood model. The conditions include local strong concavity, Lipschitz regularity of likelihood Hessians, limited model approximation error, and a sufficiently small synthetic contribution λ. The theorem assumes an infinite sample from the real distribution; it is not a universal mixing-ratio recommendation.
- **Finite samples:** Theorem 2 (Section 3.2) bounds distance from the fixed point with high probability only under an additional local assumption controlling the finite-sample retraining update’s parameter error. The authors explicitly say their stability condition is sufficient, not shown necessary, and identify finite-sampling stability as an open issue (Sections 3.1–3.2 and 6).
- **Experiments:** The empirical study uses synthetic eight-Gaussian/two-moons distributions, CIFAR-10, and FFHQ-64; model families include continuous normalizing flows and diffusion models (Section 5; Appendix G). It reports a range of behavior by dataset, model, and synthetic proportion: for EDM on CIFAR-10 the plotted run is stable over λ from 0 to 1; for EDM on FFHQ-64 the plotted stable regime is λ < 0.5 (Figure 2, p. 9). Precision/recall tradeoffs vary across model/dataset combinations (Section 5). These image/model settings do not directly test autoregressive language-model pretraining.

## Interpretation and boundaries

The paper shows a conditional result for retraining when the same original data remain available at each iteration; it does not show that new, independent observations must be acquired continuously. Retaining original examples and obtaining fresh external information are distinct interventions. The analyzed “real” samples are assumed representative of a target distribution; their retention does not establish that those samples are factual, complete, or appropriate for every later task. No universal proportion of real/synthetic data follows from the theory or experiments.
