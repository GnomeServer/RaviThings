**What it studies:** The paper proposes a stylized synthetic-data-market equilibrium, producer/trainer incentives, provenance subsidies and watermarking, with model-dependent equilibrium and welfare expressions (Secs. III–IV). It describes calibration to publicly reported licensing transactions and a claimed C4-synthetic, ten-generation benchmark, plus simulated policy interventions and scaling-law calculations (Secs. V–IX). Its own limitations say contamination dynamics are abstracted from full distributional drift, provenance detectability is exogenous, and calibration is partial (Sec. XI).

**Scope and evidentiary limit:** This is a preprint theoretical/economic model, not a study of an operational defense data mission or a command’s effectiveness. Its claimed C4-synthetic exercise is not a measurement of internet-wide contamination, a deployed frontier model, or real-world defense readiness. The public paper describes the synthetic benchmark and market calibration at a high level; the benchmark’s corpus-generation, model, and evaluation details were not independently reproduced in this documentary collection. The closed-form policy expressions are conditional on the paper’s stylized assumptions and are not a universal optimal provenance rule.

**Disposition:** Screened and cataloged as an adjacent policy/economic modeling lead, but excluded from the core empirical model-collapse findings and from any claim that a Strategic Information Command is necessary or superior.
