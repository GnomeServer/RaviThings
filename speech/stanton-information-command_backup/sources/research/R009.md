## Methods and results — original summary

Sections 2–4 (paper pp. 4–7) analyze a specified linear-regression process with a verifier modeled by a knowledge set, bias and selectivity. Filtering trades reduced variance against injected bias. Under the stated iterative procedure, the estimator tends toward the verifier's knowledge center. An unbiased verifier and a biased verifier therefore need not yield the same long-term outcome.

Section 5.2 (paper p. 9) studies conditional variational autoencoders on MNIST. Stronger verifiers can improve early image-generation results, but the paper reports plateauing and a diversity limitation: the verifier can disproportionately reject harder modes. These are outcomes in the tested model/data setup, not a universal certificate.

Section 5.3 (paper p. 10) fine-tunes SmolLM2-135M on XSUM. Generated summaries are scored with ROUGE-1 against ground-truth references; selected summaries are used for repeated retraining. Filtered retraining improves early and stabilizes, while the unfiltered baseline does not meaningfully improve in that setup. Access to reference answers is a material condition, not a capability every open-domain application possesses.

Section 6 (paper p. 10) states limitations of the well-specified linear theory and the gap to richer models and competing notions of quality/diversity. Do not interpret this paper as proving that any verifier, original-data archive, or organizational design prevents collapse at every scale.
